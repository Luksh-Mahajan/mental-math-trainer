Mental Math Tricks

A handful of shortcuts that make certain multiplications and squares much faster to do in your head.

Multiplying two numbers in the 90s

Both numbers are close to 100, so work with how far below 100 each one is. Subtract one number's "distance from 100" from the other number to get the first part of the answer, then multiply the two distances together to get the last two digits.

96 × 97 100 − 96 = 4 100 − 97 = 3 96 − 3 = 93 → first part 4 × 3 = 12 → last two digits Answer: 9312

Squaring numbers ending in 5

Take the leading digit(s), multiply by the next number up, then stick "25" on the end.

65² Leading digit: 6 6 × 7 = 42 Append 25 Answer: 4225

Using difference of squares

To multiply two numbers, find their average and their distance from that average, then compute average² minus distance². This works well when the two numbers straddle a round number.

47 × 53 Average: 50, distance: 3 50² − 3² = 2500 − 9 Answer: 2491

Multiplying by 11

For a two-digit number, add its two digits together and slot the result between them. If the sum is 10 or more, carry the 1 into the leading digit.

34 × 11 3 + 4 = 7 → 374 58 × 11 5 + 8 = 13 → write 3, carry 1 5 + 1 = 6 → 638

Multiplying by 12

Multiply by 10, then add double the original number.

34 × 12 34 × 10 = 340 34 × 2 = 68 340 + 68 = 408

Multiplying by 9

Multiply by 10, then subtract the original number.

34 × 9 34 × 10 = 340 340 − 34 = 306
